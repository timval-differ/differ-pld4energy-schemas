Use the logbook dashboards¶

The plugin has two logbook dashboards, guided forms for the logbook of one run:

  • PLD logbook for SA-PLD and LA-PLD runs;
  • Sputter logbook for SPS runs (see The sputter logbook).

They work the same way. Each saves the run as normal entries of this plugin's schemas: an SA-PLD, LA-PLD or SPS logbook, plus a Sample entry for each new or external sample. Everything they save can afterwards be viewed and edited in NOMAD as usual; see Use the PLD logbooks. This page describes the PLD logbook first.

Compared with the NOMAD entry editor, the dashboards:

  • start each run with the samples it makes or processes;
  • show only the fields that apply to your choices (system, process steps, scan type, gases, plasma, in-situ techniques, ...);
  • ask for the targets (and in the PLD logbook the laser details) only when a process step needs them;
  • only save the logbook when every required field is filled in; until then the run can be kept as a draft;
  • convert lab units (mbar, mJ, °C, mm, nm, ...) to the units NOMAD stores.

Open it¶

Log in to NOMAD first. Then open PLD logbook or Sputter logbook from NOMAD's dashboards menu, or go to <NOMAD URL>/dashboards/differ_pld4e_schemas-pld-logbook/ or <NOMAD URL>/dashboards/differ_pld4e_schemas-sputter-logbook/ (locally: http://localhost:8000/nomad-oasis/dashboards/differ_pld4e_schemas-pld-logbook/).

It opens on the start page:

  • New logbook starts an empty form.
  • Continue current logbook returns to the logbook you were working on in this browser, whether saved or not.
  • Drafts lists the drafts in your unpublished uploads; click one to continue it.
  • Recent logbooks lists your latest logbooks; click one to open it. Find a logbook… searches all logbooks you can see.

Opening another logbook or draft asks first if it would replace a logbook you are working on. Start page in the top bar (or a click on PLD logbook) takes you back; the form stays as it is.

Fill in a run¶

Work through the sections on the left; Next takes you to the next one. The number next to a section is the number of things still missing there, and a green tick means the section is complete. Missing fields are marked once you leave a section with Next, or straight away for fields you have edited.

  1. Run. Your name and email are filled in from your NOMAD login; change them if someone else does the run. Choose the PVD system: it decides which carriers, scan options and fields are offered. Enter the date and start time of the run, or press Now when you start it. They are on the lab clock (Europe/Amsterdam), whatever time zone your computer is set to, and make up the sample IDs. The run name is optional.

  2. Samples. List the samples you make or process in this run. Set Number of new and external samples to add or remove samples in one go. For each sample choose:

    • New sample (most runs): pick the substrate from the substrate database.
    • External sample: a sample prepared elsewhere, without a sample ID. Pick its substrate, or leave the substrate empty and describe it under Substrate details. Under Films already on the sample list the films it came with, from the substrate up (material and, if known, thickness). Put anything else you know about it in the comments.
    • Existing sample: a sample made earlier with a PLD4Energy system, e.g. for an extra deposition, annealing or only characterisation. Pick it from the list.

    Substrates are only chosen from the target and substrate database, which only maintainers can change (see Set up the target and substrate database). Is a substrate missing? Ask a maintainer. Maintainers see New substrate in the database below the samples; it asks for the material name and its chemical formula (e.g. SrTiO3), which makes the samples searchable by element, and saves it in the database upload. New and external samples get a sample ID system_yyyy-mm-dd_hh:mm_S## (S01, S02, ... in the order of the list, after the samples already saved: a removed sample's number is not given again) and a sample name made from the substrate and the films grown on it, e.g. SrTiO3_YSZ-20nm_SrRuO3-50nm. The films come from the deposition steps (see below), so the sample name changes once the (desired) film thickness is entered under Process steps. Existing samples keep their own ID. 3. System details. The substrate carrier and the base pressure of the main chamber. LA-PLD also asks for the particle reduction plate. 4. Targets and 5. Laser details. Only needed when a process step uses the laser (pre-ablation and deposition). Without such a step they show as not needed in the list on the left, and nothing entered there is saved. - Targets: pick each target in the system and its target number (its position, 1 to 5) for this run. The dashboard cannot add PLD targets: a maintainer adds a new target as a PLD Target entry in the target and substrate database upload in NOMAD, with its chemical formula, shape and size, density and the ID engraved on its back (see Reference › PLD Target). - Laser details: enter the energies before and after the chamber. The 1st window transmission is calculated as you type; switch on Enter the 1st window transmission manually to type your own value instead. 6. Process steps. Add the steps in order with the + ... buttons: pre- ablation, heating, annealing, pulsed laser deposition and cool-down. Each step can be moved up or down, removed, and folded away with a click on its title. A run without deposition (e.g. only annealing or only characterisation) is fine. - Time: the start and end time of each step, in 24-hour hh:mm (e.g. 14:05; 1405 or 14.05 is turned into that), as in the system log, so the pressure, temperature, ... of the step can be found in the logs later. The date is that of the run; a time after midnight is taken as the next day. Both times are optional. A new step starts at the end time of the step before it; change it if there was a pause. An end time before the start time is an error. A step that starts before the step before it ends gets a warning, but can still be saved. A prefilled step or a recipe does not copy the times. - Background gases: choose UHV to record only the chamber pressure, or Background gas and tick each gas to fill in its set and read-out flow (at least one gas must be ticked). All gases share one valve, so its position (0 closed to 1000 open) is entered once per step. - Target stage: start x, y and z position, offsets, scan width and scan speed in x and y. - Temperature control (heating, annealing, deposition and cool-down): the fields follow the Temperature control panel of the system software. Tick Fix output and Drift compensation as set there. A heating step also has Ramp on: its ramp rate is only asked for when the ramp is on. Annealing and cool-down steps always ask for the ramp rate. Deposition steps have no ramp. - SA-PLD: choose the heating mode. Laser heating (PID) takes the setpoint in °C and the ramp rate in °C/min, and asks for the heater output (%) once the setpoint is reached. Laser heating (power) takes the setpoint in % and the ramp rate in %/min. Also note the laser output limitation set with the knob (50% unless changed). - LA-PLD: setpoint in °C and ramp rate in °C/min, plus the set and actual voltage and current of the main heater power supply. - Pulsed laser deposition: a new deposition step starts with the target, target stage, gases and laser settings of the pre-ablation step before it; change what differs. Enter the film thickness, in nm or, with Thickness given in set to pulses, as a number of laser pulses (the sample name then shows e.g. YSZ-1500p). Each deposition step grows one film, of its target's material, on its samples, and these films make up the sample names. In the SA-PLD a deposition covers the samples ticked under Samples covered (all by default): untick those shielded by a sample mask. In the LA-PLD choose the sample. The target and heater stage fields follow the Manual movement and Define scan pattern dialogs of the system software; choose the heater scan type (Polar scan is the default) to get its scan fields. 7. In-situ techniques. Optional. For each measurement: + Add measurement, choose the sample, tick the process steps it was taken during, then switch on each technique you used and add its data files. A file field takes several files of its format, e.g. a series of RHEED .png images. Files can also be added later: a technique without files is saved as awaiting data. A file never replaces another one: a name used twice gets a number, e.g. step1 (2).txt. 8. System logfiles. Optional: add the .txt logfiles exported from the TSST software. 9. Review and save. Lists everything that is still missing; click an item to jump to the field. Choose an upload (or + New upload) and save.

Saving creates the Sample entries first and then the logbook, and shows NOMAD's processing messages. After saving, the form stays open: saving again updates the same logbook and its samples.

Drafts: continue on another computer¶

A run often spans several computers, and a logbook can only be saved once it is complete. To keep an unfinished run, use Save draft (top bar or Review and save). It needs the system, date and start time, and an upload.

  • The draft is stored in the upload as pld_drafts/<run>.draft.json (sps_drafts/ for the sputter logbook), together with the in-situ files you picked. It is not an entry: it does not show up in searches and does not count as logged. Only the saved logbook does.
  • On another computer, the start page lists the drafts in your unpublished uploads; pick one to carry on.
  • If someone saved the same draft elsewhere after you opened it, you are asked whether to overwrite it or to load their version. Cancel keeps your form and does not save the draft.
  • Saving the complete logbook deletes the draft.

Independently of drafts, the form is kept in your browser while you work, so you can close the page and continue later on the same computer. It is kept per NOMAD user: on a shared computer, the next user does not get your form. Files you chose but did not save yet are not kept: choose them again. New logbook on the start page clears the form.

Export as PDF¶

Export as PDF (top bar, or Review and save) turns the logbook in the form into a report and downloads it as a PDF file, named after the run. The report lists every section and step with the values that apply, in lab units. Sections the steps do not need are left out.

Any logbook can be exported, saved or not. Unless it is a complete logbook that was saved to NOMAD, the report says DRAFT / INCOMPLETE: this is not a logged logbook at the top, with the number of things still missing. The PDF is saved on your computer only; the logbook in NOMAD remains the record.

Add in-situ data later¶

In-situ data often has to be collected from other computers after the run. Save the logbook without the files: the techniques show as awaiting data in the dashboard and in NOMAD. Later, open the logbook from the start page, add the files under In-situ techniques and save again. The same goes for the system logfiles.

Logbooks saved before measurements had a sample and process steps open with their techniques as one measurement: choose its sample and steps before saving again.

Repeat a run (recipe)¶

Choose the PVD system, then under Process steps click Load steps from a recipe… and pick an earlier logbook of the same system. Its process steps are added after your current steps; if you have not selected any targets yet, its targets are taken over too. Samples are not copied: the steps apply to the samples of this run (in the LA-PLD, choose the sample of each deposition step). Neither are the step times, comments or read-out values (gas flow read-out, actual heater voltage and current) of the recipe run: fill them in for this run. The sputter logbook does not record which recipe its steps came from; saving says so.

Change a saved logbook¶

Opening a saved logbook from the start page loads it into the form. Saving overwrites it and updates the Sample entries it created; samples from other runs are never changed. A sample keeps its ID once its entry exists, even if you change the date or time of the run. The logbook must be in an upload you can edit (not published).

What the form does not show is kept: values added to the logbook (outside its process steps, which come from the form as a whole) or to its Sample entries in NOMAD's entry editor (for example a lab ID, tags or references) stay when you save again. What the form shows comes from the form, also when it is empty there: the comments of the run and of a sample are the description of the logbook and of the Sample entry. Sample entries are updated without checking for changes made in NOMAD. If the logbook was changed in NOMAD after you opened it, saving asks whether to overwrite it or to save the form as a new logbook. A logbook with process steps the dashboard cannot show is not overwritten: save it as a new logbook, or edit it in NOMAD.

The sputter logbook¶

The Sputter logbook is filled in like the PLD logbook, with these differences:

  1. Run. The PVD system is always SPS. Sample IDs are SPS_yyyy-mm-dd_hh:mm_S##.
  2. Samples. As in the PLD logbook. The sample names come from the sputtering steps, e.g. Si_Ta-50nm_Ti-10nm.
  3. System details. The substrate carrier (4" carrier SPS, 1 cm² sub plate or Fixed stage) and the main chamber pressure. With the fixed stage, say whether a mask is used and which one.
  4. Targets. Only needed when a step sputters (pre-sputtering or sputtering). Pick each target and its position: positions 1 and 2 take 2" targets, position 3 a 4" target. Targets come from the sputter target database, separate from the PLD targets. Maintainers see New target in the database, which asks for the chemical formula and the diameter in inch. NOMAD warns on save when a target does not fit its position.
  5. Process steps. Pre-sputtering, heating, sputtering and cool-down. For a multilayer, add a pre-sputtering and sputtering step per layer.
    • Plasma: choose DC, RF or DC+RF. DC asks for the voltage U, RF for the load capacitance CL, DC+RF for both. The current I and the DC bias are optional; the power is required.
    • Power ramps (ramp up, ramp down) are entered in W per 5 minutes, as in the lab. NOMAD shows them in W/min: 10 W/5 min is 2 W/min.
    • Sputtering: a new sputtering step starts with the target, gases and plasma of the pre-sputtering step before it. Enter the heater position (x, y, z, spin), heater rotation, shutter, sputter time and the film thickness. A sputtering step covers the samples ticked under Samples covered (all by default): untick those shielded by a sample mask. Sputtering currently does not support a heating step.
  6. General comments. Anything else about the run.

There is no laser and no in-situ section.

Good to know¶

  • Required fields are marked with *. Comments are optional. A field with only spaces counts as empty.
  • Numbers take a dot or one comma for decimals: 1.5 and 1,5 are the same. A comma that may separate thousands, as in 12,000 or 1,500, is refused: write 12000, or 12 if twelve was meant.
  • The dashboard checks the form again on the server before anything is saved.
  • Targets and substrates come only from the target and substrate database upload; View the database in NOMAD (Edit for maintainers) opens it.
  • New targets and substrates added to the database in NOMAD appear when you start a new logbook or open Samples or Targets; Refresh lists reloads them at any time.
  • Targets and substrates that are switched off as out of stock in NOMAD are not offered. A logbook that already uses one keeps it, marked (out of stock).
  • A new logbook never overwrites an existing file: if a logbook with the same system, date, time and run name, or a sample with the same sample ID, already exists in the upload, saving stops with a message.
