Use the PLD logbook dashboard¶

The PLD logbook dashboard is a guided form for the logbook of an SA-PLD or LA-PLD run. It saves the run as normal entries of this plugin's schemas: an SA-PLD or LA-PLD logbook, plus a Sample entry for each new or external sample. Everything it saves can afterwards be viewed and edited in NOMAD as usual; see Use the PLD logbooks.

Compared with the NOMAD entry editor, the dashboard:

  • starts each run with the samples it makes or processes;
  • shows only the fields that apply to your choices (system, process steps, scan type, gases, in-situ techniques, ...);
  • asks for the laser details and targets only when a process step needs the laser;
  • only saves the logbook when every required field is filled in; until then the run can be kept as a draft;
  • converts lab units (mbar, mJ, °C, mm, nm, ...) to the units NOMAD stores.

Open it¶

Log in to NOMAD first. Then open PLD logbook from NOMAD's dashboards menu, or go to <NOMAD URL>/dashboards/differ_pld4e_schemas-pld-logbook/ (locally: http://localhost:8000/nomad-oasis/dashboards/differ_pld4e_schemas-pld-logbook/).

It opens on the start page:

  • New logbook starts an empty form.
  • Continue current logbook returns to the logbook you were working on in this browser, whether saved or not.
  • Drafts lists the drafts in your unpublished uploads; click one to continue it.
  • Recent logbooks lists your latest logbooks; click one to open it. Find a logbook… searches all logbooks you can see.

Opening another logbook or draft asks first if it would replace a logbook you are working on. Start page in the top bar (or a click on PLD logbook) takes you back; the form stays as it is.

Fill in a run¶

Work through the sections on the left; Next takes you to the next one. The number next to a section is the number of things still missing there, and a green tick means the section is complete. Missing fields are marked once you leave a section with Next, or straight away for fields you have edited.

  1. Run. Your name and email are filled in from your NOMAD login; change them if someone else does the run. Choose the PVD system: it decides which carriers, scan options and fields are offered. The date and start time are today and now unless you change them. The run name is optional.

  2. Samples. List the samples you make or process in this run. Set Number of new and external samples to add or remove samples in one go. For each sample choose:

    • New sample (most runs): pick the substrate from the substrate database.
    • External sample: a sample prepared elsewhere, without a sample ID. Pick its substrate, or leave the substrate empty and describe it under Substrate details. Under Films already on the sample list the films it came with, from the substrate up (material and, if known, thickness). Put anything else you know about it in the comments.
    • Existing sample: a sample made earlier with a PLD4Energy system, e.g. for an extra deposition, annealing or only characterisation. Pick it from the list.

    A substrate or target that is not in the database yet can be added with New substrate in the database or New target in the database, below the samples. New and external samples get a sample ID system_yyyy-mm-dd_hh:mm_S## (S01, S02, ... in the order of the list) and a sample name made from the substrate and the films grown on it, e.g. SrTiO3_YSZ-20nm_SrRuO3-50nm. The films come from the deposition steps (see below), so the name is complete once the steps are filled in. Existing samples keep their own ID. 3. System details. The substrate carrier and the base pressure of the main chamber. LA-PLD also asks for the particle reduction plate. 4. Targets and 5. Laser details. Only needed when a process step uses the laser (pre-ablation and deposition). Without such a step they show as not needed in the list on the left, and nothing entered there is saved. - Targets: pick each target in the system and its target number (its position, 1 to 5) for this run. A target that is not in the database yet can be added with New target in the database. - Laser details: enter the energies before and after the chamber. The 1st window transmission is calculated as you type; switch on Enter the 1st window transmission manually to type your own value instead. 6. Process steps. Add the steps in order with the + ... buttons: pre- ablation, heating, annealing, pulsed laser deposition and cool-down. Each step can be moved up or down, removed, and folded away with a click on its title. A run without deposition (e.g. only annealing or only characterisation) is fine. - Background gases: choose UHV to record only the chamber pressure, or Background gas and tick each gas to fill in its set and read-out flow. All gases share one valve, so its position is entered once per step. - Target stage: start x, y and z position, offsets, scan width and scan speed in x and y. - Heating and annealing: note the heater output (%) once the setpoint is reached. - Pulsed laser deposition: a new deposition step starts with the target, target stage, gases and laser settings of the pre-ablation step before it; change what differs. Enter the film thickness: each deposition step grows one film, of its target's material, on its samples, and these films make up the sample names. In the SA-PLD a deposition covers all samples loaded in the chamber; switch on Substrate stage scan for its scan width and speed. In the LA-PLD choose the sample, and the heater scan type (Polar scan is the default). 7. In-situ techniques. Tick each technique you used. Add its data files now or later: a technique without files is saved as awaiting data. 8. Review and save. Lists everything that is still missing; click an item to jump to the field. Choose an upload (or + New upload) and save.

Saving creates the Sample entries first and then the logbook, and shows NOMAD's processing messages. After saving, the form stays open: saving again updates the same logbook and its samples.

Drafts: continue on another computer¶

A run often spans several computers, and a logbook can only be saved once it is complete. To keep an unfinished run, use Save draft (top bar or Review and save). It needs the system, date and start time, and an upload.

  • The draft is stored in the upload as pld_drafts/<run>.draft.json, together with the in-situ files you picked. It is not an entry: it does not show up in searches and does not count as logged. Only the saved logbook does.
  • On another computer, the start page lists the drafts in your unpublished uploads; pick one to carry on.
  • If someone saved the same draft elsewhere after you opened it, you are asked whether to overwrite it or to load their version.
  • Saving the complete logbook deletes the draft.

Independently of drafts, the form is kept in your browser while you work, so you can close the page and continue later on the same computer. Files you chose but did not save yet are not kept: choose them again. New logbook on the start page clears the form.

Export as PDF¶

Export as PDF (top bar, or Review and save) turns the logbook in the form into a report and downloads it as a PDF file, named after the run. The report lists every section and step with the values that apply, in lab units. Sections the steps do not need are left out.

Any logbook can be exported, saved or not. Unless it is a complete logbook that was saved to NOMAD, the report says DRAFT / INCOMPLETE: this is not a logged logbook at the top, with the number of things still missing. The PDF is saved on your computer only; the logbook in NOMAD remains the record.

Add in-situ data later¶

In-situ data often has to be collected from other computers after the run. Save the logbook without the files: the techniques show as awaiting data in the dashboard and in NOMAD. Later, open the logbook from the start page, add the files under In-situ techniques and save again.

Repeat a run (recipe)¶

Choose the PVD system, then under Process steps click Load steps from a recipe… and pick an earlier logbook of the same system. Its process steps are added after your current steps; if you have not selected any targets yet, its targets are taken over too. Samples are not copied: the steps apply to the samples of this run (in the LA-PLD, choose the sample of each deposition step).

Change a saved logbook¶

Opening a saved logbook from the start page loads it into the form. Saving overwrites it and updates the Sample entries it created; samples from other runs are never changed. A sample keeps its ID once its entry exists, even if you change the date or time of the run. The logbook must be in an upload you can edit (not published).

Good to know¶

  • Required fields are marked with *. Comments are optional.
  • The dashboard checks the form again on the server before anything is saved.
  • New targets and substrates added elsewhere in NOMAD appear when you start a new logbook or open Samples or Targets; Refresh lists reloads them at any time.
  • Targets and substrates that are switched off as out of stock in NOMAD are not offered. A logbook that already uses one keeps it, marked (out of stock).
  • A new logbook never overwrites an existing file: if a logbook with the same system, date, time and run name, or a sample with the same sample ID, already exists in the upload, saving stops with a message.
